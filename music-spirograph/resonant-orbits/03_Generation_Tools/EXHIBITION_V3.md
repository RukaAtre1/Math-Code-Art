# Exhibition V3 design

Three persistent spirographs respond to rhythm, dynamics and timbre. A fourth pitch-related spirograph fades in during sustained multi-pitch chroma activity. This is a harmonic-activity approximation, not chord identification.

Each artwork draws all of its bodies with one curve family, chosen as an artistic match in the same way as its palette. Every family is a double rotor: three arms (ring to rotor 1, rotor 1 to rotor 2, rotor 2 to the pen) with long secondary arms and slow relative speeds, which give the original drawing its density. The pieces differ by mechanism: knot garland (Lunar Drift; rotor 2 divides the ring a whole number of times and the two small arms match, so every loop passes through a few bright knots), crystal star (Crystal Bloom; a small first rotor gives sharp six- and eight-point stars), eddy wheel (Mountain Current; both rotors roll outside, a round rim around a ring of small eddies), corona (Dawn of an Orbit; a slow second rotor leaves a ringed sun disc inside radiating points) and waltz (Celestial Waltz; slow wide gear ratios with three-fold symmetry). Gear ratios are whole numbers, as on a toy spirograph, so each figure is one closed curve of 8 to 23 main-arm turns. Because it closes exactly, accumulated passes retrace the same lines and only musical changes add new ones; the original double rotor's non-whole ratios would fill a solid disc within minutes of accumulation. The role mapping still produces R, r1, r2 and d. Families read them as multipliers of each body's starting values: overall extent, rotor 1's arm length, a twist of that arm, and the pen arm's length, which opens knots, lengthens star points or enlarges eddies. Music changes arm lengths and phases, never gear ratios, so the figure stays whole while it morphs. A motif (gear set and symmetry) changes only after a 15-second dwell, when the slow chroma-change cue is high, through a 2.5-second cross-fade. The original double rotor remains available as `--family double_rotor`, and deliverables rendered before 2026-10-17 use it. For audio without a curated family, `--family auto` takes the family of the most similar exhibition recording by whole-recording descriptors. It does not recognize genre, meter or emotion.

With the double rotor, rhythm and dynamics are recomputed every frame as a moving 22% window of 2,000 points, and timbre and optional pitch keep 2.8 seconds of continuous strokes. With a curve family, all four bodies draw with a pen and every stroke stays for the whole recording: the figure builds up from a blank canvas, completes in about six seconds at typical pen speed, and keeps layering, so the last frame records the whole piece. The newest 0.6 seconds are vector strokes with a glowing head; older strokes settle into a per-body raster layer at half width and 40% opacity, which rotates, scales and moves with its body. A hidden fourth body keeps its strokes and shows them again when it returns. Family print works accumulate the whole recording and show its final frame. All visible strokes rotate and translate together; family figures rotate 1.5 times as fast as double-rotor ink voices. Fixed collision circles keep bodies separated and constrain curve size.

Geometry parameters, rotation, movement, stroke width and color respond to onset, RMS, bass, centroid, bandwidth, flux, beat and chroma. Full-recording profiles and a seed set reproducible initial geometry. Periodic artistic perturbations supplement audio mapping. Beat estimates need not match score beats, and roles do not imply isolated instruments.

Each theme has a bounded palette. Layered color combines slow musical change with immediate features; continuous strokes retain their original colors. Photographic backgrounds require source and license sidecars. Procedural backgrounds remain available as a fallback.

Print works use one primary figure and two supporting voices, rendered directly at 6000 × 3375 pixels with 300 DPI metadata. Double-rotor prints select an active moment preceded by eight seconds of real feature integration; family prints accumulate the whole recording.

Run commands are in the project README. Original notebooks and implementations remain in the sibling original-version directory. Historical source snapshots are retained in 04_Archive.
